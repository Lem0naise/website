The lamp behind Norbert Oek's shoulder gave off a dry, electrical hum, casting his shadow long across the oak desk. Norbert was spinning his pencil between his fingers, a staple habit in the office.

He was alone in the office, as it was past eight on a Friday, and the rest of the registry floor was dead. Norbert liked to work late into the night, as the silence helped him focus. He stared back down at the paper in front of him, rubbing his eyes with his sleeve. The uppercase title *Upland Grain Trust* stared back at him. The massive agriculture cooperative filed documents almost every month, and Norbert had never noticed any discrepancies in them before. In this office, a document with the thick blue signature of Senator Dorde Palic was practically an automatic approval. He flicked through the pile of papers stacked to his right. The *Trust* had been operating since 1982, and Norbert himself had authorised almost forty million Krejts in the last year to be allocated for land re-development under its oversight by Palic, a former agronomist. 

There were routinely new land parcels assigned to the *Trust*, and it was not out of the ordinary for Norbert to sign off authorisations from the District IX government allocating yet more greenfield for development. Today, however, buried beneath thirty pages of routine crop-rotation tax exemptions, was a single new land allocation: twenty-five square kilometers of forest just north of the city of Kromine. 

It was an absurd request to slip into a routine stack. A junior clerk would have stamped it and gone home for the weekend. Not Norbert, though, as for better or for worse, he was incapable of letting things go. His wife, Klara, would tell you it was decidedly for worse. 

He pulled a brass magnifying glass from his drawer and leaned closer over the document, flipping the page over to see the grid coordinates the cooperative had submitted. There was no crop index, no silo assignment, just two words typed into the margin: *Wheat farm*. 

Norbert turned around and pulled from the filing cabinet a thick red book. Its cover was dust-free and clearly routinely used. He opened it to three-quarters through, flicked forwards a couple of pages, and found the grid he was looking for, the fifty square kilometers between the town of Brodska and the district capital of Kromine. His freshly-filed nail made a thin line in the paper as he followed the 14th grid row across the page until he reached D-14. The coordinates did not seem to sit on farmable soil, in fact there was a sheer slate cliff halfway down the map. Norbert picked up his pencil and opened a new page in his pocket notebook. He made a rough sketch of the grid, and circled a large pine forest, several tall hills and the disused quarry by the cliff, all of which looked to be within the requested parcel of land. The atlas made a clapping sound as he shut it, it slotted neatly back into the cabinet behind him. 

He looked up from his desk, blinking in the fading light, to glance at the wall clock. It was late. If he filed a disrepancy report, his supervisor Eward wouldn't even pick it up until Monday morning, by which time, Norbert was sure to be buried in even more paperwork. (In all actuality, that was quite an exciting thought for Norbert.)

He collected all the papers from his desk into his briefcase, and took his grey overcoat from where it hung by the door and locked the office behind him.

The night air outside was biting and smelled faintly of coal smoke. As he took the short walk down the hill to his house, the plan for tomorrow began to formulate in his head: an early morning drive into the ridge forest above Brodska. Just a drive, just to see how someone could build a wheat farm on a slate cliff. 

He unlocked his front door with a gentle jingle of keys, and slid quietly into bed beside his sleeping wife.

Character Profiles

Emil Varg (The Clerk): A quiet, meticulous 35-year-old land registry clerk in the fading upland town of Brodska. Emil is not a hero; he is a man who finds peace in the absolute, unyielding order of physical archives. He believes that if a tree falls in the forest, it only truly exists once its timber rights are filed in triplicate.
Klara Varg (His Wife): Pragmatic, deeply loving, but utterly exhausted by the economic stagnation of the uplands. She watches Brodska’s youth drain southward to the Kromine tech factories. Klara is the pragmatic voice of survival—she isn't corrupt, but she knows what it costs to buy coal for the winter.
Ari Stov (The Young Entrepreneur): Only 18 years old, but already displaying the terrifying, cold charisma of a generational technocrat. He doesn't look like a villain; he is polite, exceptionally logical, and speaks of the future not in terms of politics, but in "data efficiency" and "network paths."

Scene-by-Scene Story Outline
Act I: The Ghost Coordinate

The Setting: The damp, high-ceilinged municipal records office in Brodska. The smell of old ink, tea, and coal dust.
The Hook: While auditing the tax-exempt land grants of the Upland Grain Trust (a massive agricultural cooperative backed by Senator Dorde Palic), Emil finds a glaring anomaly. A massive, 20-kilometer parcel of land on the ridge is registered to a latitude coordinate that mathematically cannot exist within the standard Kresimirian grid system.
The System Puzzle: The database should have flagged and rejected the entry. However, Emil discovers a custom, unauthorized cryptographic "patch" in the database that forced the system to accept the impossible coordinate. On paper, the Upland Grain Trust is tax-exempt for "mechanized commons", but the coordinates point to a blank space on the map.
The Decision: Rather than filing a routine flag (which his supervisor would likely bury), Emil’s quiet obsession with ledger accuracy forces him to physically investigate.

Act II: The Silent Ridge

The Setting: The misty, grey-wooded pine forests above Brodska.
The Investigation: Emil walks the mountain paths to where the coordinates should logically fall if the coordinate error was a simple typo. He stops at a lonely upland farm. He asks an elderly, weathered peasant where the "Upland Grain Trust Silos" are. The farmer laughs bitterly—there are no silos here. Just empty state-owned forest land.
The Discovery: Emil pushes deeper into the restricted state woodlands. The quiet of the forest is broken by the low, mechanical rumble of a ditch-witch. He hides behind the pines and witnesses a bizarre sight: there are no crops or grain. Instead, a disciplined crew of unmarked private contractors is digging a deep, narrow trench, burying thick, black, heavily insulated fiber-optic cables directly into the mountain dirt.

Act III: The Offer in the Mist

The Confrontation: Emil is quietly intercepted by two silent security guards, but before things turn violent, a young Ari Stov steps out of the fog. Stov is polite, offering Emil a thermos of hot tea.
The Logical Revelation (The Asimov "Aha!" Moment): Stov doesn't deny the fraud. He explains the system logic to Emil with chilling clarity. Senator Palic’s Upland Grain Trust is indeed a paper shell. They are using the tax-exempt "ghost coordinates" to hide the physical path of Kresimiria's first high-speed digital trunk line.
The Pitch: Stov explains that "data is Kresimiria's new territory". The physical cable they are burying right now will connect Sinj directly to the Kromine foundries, laying the groundwork for what will eventually become the KresiX smart grid and the YakaSys network.
The Hook/Offer: Stov recognizes Emil's brilliance for spotting the database patch. Instead of threatening him, Stov offers him a way out of his dying town: a lucrative "consulting contract" with his newly founded firm, Stov Informational Systems. The salary is ten times Emil's annual clerk wage. All Emil has to do is go back to the office, re-classify the "ghost coordinate" as a routine clerical transposal error, and permanently archive the file.

Act IV: The Kitchen Table (The Symons Dilemma)

The Setting: Emil’s modest, cold kitchen in Brodska. The hum of a cheap, flickering electric light.
The Domestic Tension: Emil sits across from Klara. He places the signed, un-filed discrepancy report on the table next to Stov's glossy, high-paying corporate contract.
The Psychological Conflict: Klara is the catalyst. She doesn't care about fiber-optic cables or Senator Palic's corrupt land-grabbing. She cares about their leaking roof, the rising cost of medicine, and the fact that they are slowly rotting in a town with no future. She asks Emil: "Who are you protecting by filing that report? The state that ignores us? The farmers who will be bought out anyway?"
The Chilling Climax: Emil looks at the contract. He realizes the horrifying truth: if he takes Stov's money, he secures a wealthy, comfortable life in the Kromine "New East". But he also realizes that the very digital network he is helping Stov hide—the one he will help build—is designed to monitor and control every phone, transaction, and citizen in the Republic. By signing the contract to escape the system, he is literally signing his name to the creation of Kresimiria's inescapable digital panopticon.
The Ending: Emil picks up his pen. The story ends on a lingering, psychological note of suspense as his hand hovers over the signature line.

Key Historical Connections to Reference

The Brodska Context: Frame Brodska as a fading, quiet, conservative upland town. The young workers are already leaving, and the locals are fiercely skeptical of Sinj.
The "Old Iron" vs. "New Tech" Transition: The story captures the exact historical moment where the old, corrupt physical land-grabbing of Senator Palic (who was eventually prosecuted in 1993) is being utilized as the launchpad for the high-tech, digital surveillance state of Ari Stov.
The Seeds of the Panopticon: The black cables Emil finds in the woods are the literal physical veins that will eventually host the Guardian Daemon and the KresiX operating system.
