2. The Soot-Rider’s Leap (A High-Octane Sports Drama)

    The Setup: A story centered around the annual Vjetrusa Cup—the unpowered, high-altitude hand-gliding race held in the jagged, ice-blue peaks of the Severni Range. The protagonist is a pilot for the working-class Bistrica Soot-Riders.
    The Conflict: Unlike the golden, polymer-coated gliders of the Sinj elite, the Soot-Riders' rig is a "patchwork monstrosity" built from scavenged industrial canvas, repurposed timber, and scrap metal. To win a life-changing, year-long exemption from military conscription, the pilot must execute the highly illegal and lethal "Soot-Blind" maneuver—flying headfirst into a blinding, freezing glacial fog bank to blindside their wealthy opponents.
    The Mood: The howling, violent winds of the northern glaciers, the smell of canvas and grease, and the breathless, terrifying joy of unpowered flight high above the smog of the coal mines.

3. The Shadow of the 1993 Moraviski Crisis (A Gritty Political Drama)

    The Setup: Set in Sprodvice or Brod Moravice during the explosive March Riots of 1993. The protagonist is a junior, non-violent coordinator for the Bosken Liberation Front (BLF).
    The Conflict: Following the arrest of Senator Kristrad Bronstein, Sprodvice has been placed under a total military blockade. While armored CIA vehicles patrol the cobblestone streets of the Old Quarter, the protagonist is tasked with smuggling medical supplies to an underground community clinic while hiding a radical socialist organizer fleeing the OAB. They have to navigate the city's tense, divided geography—slipping between the gleaming, federal-funded "New Zone" and the neglected, crumbling ruins of the historic center.
    The Mood: Thick smoke from street fires, distant mortar thuds along the border, whispering in Pravoslavic churches, and the constant fear of a knock on the door.

4. Banning the Beat on Albion (A Retro-Dystopian Hermit Kingdom Story)

    The Setup: Set in your newly conceived, hyper-isolated, English-speaking Republic of Albion. The protagonist is a young municipal worker in the brutalist, rain-swept capital of Port Sterling.

They must listen in absolute silence, knowing that the secret police are patrolling the wet cobblestones below with analog signal-detectors.

She must use the absolute, rigid logic of Kresimirian canon law to outwit the auditor without ever admitting she found the bug.

    The Topic: The Media Licensing Authority's "Yellow Ban" (1956–1961).
    The Hook: During the height of the "Iron Era," the MLA has banned the visual marketing of any food that displays the color yellow. He must use the rigid, literal text of the Primum to logically prove that the ripening of fruit is a mandatory, uncontrollable act of Lord Kresimir—meaning that prosecuting him for the yellow peel is technically committing state-mandated heresy against the Creator's own natural law.
