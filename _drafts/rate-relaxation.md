Nobody just 'goes for a run' anymore. You have to log it on Strava, take your Apple watch, post the route on your Instagram story. If it's not public, it didn't happen, right? But we shouldn't need to track our hobbies. As someone who is [self-admittedly](../favourites-me) obsessed with data entry and analysis, this was a hard realisation for me to come to.

### Micro-grief

I'm not blaming us, though. I think the reason we do this is *micro-grief*. It's uncomfortable for humans to experience things which are fleeting. We naturally try to hold on to things and keep them in our life, whether they're memories or physical things. This is what causes hoarders to start building up their obsessive collections - nowadays we have the ability to keep everything with us, so why wouldn't we?

If we turn relaxation into a trackable metric however, while it may give you some analytic takeaways, I feel it inevitably detracts from the act itself, and the fun. If I am constantly focussing on *optimising*, I lose the enjoyment and the whimsy of the act itself.
