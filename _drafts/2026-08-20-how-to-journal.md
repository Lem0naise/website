I recently logged my six thousandth diary entry, which marks just over 8 years since I started writing entries at least daily. That means I have a record of my life going back to when I was twelve, in August 2018. 

Some of the first entries are barely entries at all. They are mostly evidence that I had downloaded Daylio, my new journalling app, and was enjoying pressing its buttons. There are now thousands and thousands of these tiny moods, activities, one-line summaries and occasional paragraphs of days I would otherwise forget. My diary covers school, friends, family, moving house, university, exams, relationships, and so many past thoughts that I may sometimes prefer to forget.

![](/assets/imgs/dayliocalendar.png)

## Your journal is for yourself

When some people imagine journalling, they often picture a leather notebook, a fountain pen, and a candlelit room. When some people imagine a diary, they think of something which has to be polished, publishable, like a memoir. I disagree with both of these. If you hold yourself to such a high standard, you would write enthusiastically for three evenings, miss the fourth, and then feel guilty every time you see the notebook for the next six months.

I use the app [Daylio](https://daylio.net), which is specifically designed to allow 'bad' diary entries. I can literally log a day as 'Tired' and write 'played games' and that could be it. Sure, it's not a very useful data point, but it's a journal entry nonetheless, and representative of my mood at that time.

The very very last thing a journal should ever be is more homework or stress in your life. It should be an outlet for your most private thoughts and moods. It should not feel like an obligation. You do not have to write every single thing that happened, every single meal you had, every single person you saw. You can just write the mood you went to bed feeling. That's perfectly okay.

## Don't limit yourself

This comes back again to the idea of a loose structure. You don't need to limit yourself to one entry per day, I definitely don't. Humans' moods change frequently! You can wake up feeling terrible and have a shockingly good day, or the inverse. 

Reducing an entire day to one averaged mood is a ridiculous compression, of course. If you slept terribly but had a great evening, that 'meh' day may be very different to a completely boring day later in the year. 

But that's the amazing thing about keeping a journal, and in this case a digitised one - your journal is yours to keep and analyse if you want to. There are so many cool stats you can get from the pure volume of data that builds up. 

![](/assets/imgs/spidergraph.png)

## Some insights I've found

It is also a very useful 'hindsight' tool. If you find yourself falling into patterns, negative or positive, having past data makes incredibly easy to track. 

A terrible evening is often followed by a completely ordinary morning, and a week that felt utterly endless becomes four red squares surrounded by a sea of improving yellows and greens. This is useful knowledge to have when you are stuck spiraling inside one of the squares!

There are of course entries which I would rather be kept private - not necessarily scandalous - just unfinished, or contain a view that past me held that present me no longer agrees with. But I don't go back and edit past entries - because I find it really interesting to see the progression of thoughts. We are inherently unreliable narrators of our own life, so if you have a place where you can be as brutally honest as you can about everything, looking back on it later is extraordinarily insightful. 


## But my life is so boring!!

Firstly, see [above](#your-journal-is-for-yourself). Secondly, some entries are meant to be boring! If you had an incredibly lazy Sunday and just did laundry, that's okay! Write "lazy sunday, did laundry". If, years later, you have a depressive episode and cannot fathom the strength to do laundry, you can look back on boring days and see that you do indeed have the ability to do laundry, and you are a person who has had boring days, and can have them again. 'Boring' is sometimes a positive. And you wouldn't know that, if you hadn't written that down.
